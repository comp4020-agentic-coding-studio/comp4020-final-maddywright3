# C10 feedback and what came of it

## Two-person test from the URL alone

Two people, each in their own Chrome profile, were given only /readme/. I didn't touch the app and narrated from flyctl logs.

*Could I tell them apart?* Yes. Each showed up as a different short hash, and no raw IDs, cookies or codes appeared:

{"time":"2026-11-15T06:55:38.180Z","action":"page_view","lecture":"demo","who":"5cbf9db8"}
{"time":"2026-11-15T06:55:46.883Z","action":"tap","lecture":"demo","who":"926e1fce"}

*What they said:*
- Two windows with a button and a number don't show what the app is for. There's no lecture to be lost in.
- They wanted to see how many other people were lost, and at which point.
- They wanted a way to say why they were lost.

## My own two-tab test

- After one tap the button stayed on "You're counted", with nothing saying when it would unlock.
- Two tabs in one browser count as one person. That's by design, but nothing on screen says so.

## Review of the C10 build and the live site

- *The threshold hid text, not data.* Below min_present the gauge message was blank, but the stream still carried the live count and total, the page's total still moved, and the lecturer's timeline still updated.
- *The repo was still private*, so CI had never run the checks against the Docker image.
- *The README's premise had no source.* "People don't put their hand up" carried the argument unsupported.
- *The gauge's aria-live region announced every change*, including people arriving and leaving, which is the opposite of calm for a screen reader user.
- *No message when the stream drops*, so on a slow connection the gauge goes stale silently.
- *No load test* with a full room.
- *Ten wrong lecturer codes lock out everyone* for that lecture, because the limit is per lecture.
- */new has no limit*, so the 1 GB volume could be filled on purpose.
